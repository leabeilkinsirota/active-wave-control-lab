{S:34}Acoustic Illusion of a Rigid Object for a Moving Emitter via Equivalent Surface Control
[We report a three-dimensional acoustic illusion of a rigid object illuminated by an emitter moving along an arbitrary trajectory. The object is absent: control sources are arranged around its location, the closed surface they define being termed the equivalent surface. Driven by the field the object would have scattered onto this surface, the sources radiate, by the Kirchhoff–Helmholtz representation, that same field beyond it. The control signals follow in closed form from a time-dependent physical optics formulation that we derive for a moving emitter, in which the motion enters analytically through the acoustic travel times, so that the scattering problem need not be re-solved at each emitter position, nor the propagation domain discretized. For objects too large to enclose, the signals are mapped onto an internal surface, an option whose advantages and penalties are discussed. Numerical simulations validate the method in both configurations.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21649)

{S:34}Acoustic Scattered Field Calculation for a Moving Source Using Time-Dependent Physical Optics
[We present a time-dependent physical optics (TDPO) formulation for computing the acoustic scattered field of a stationary object in response to a moving source in three-dimensional space. Due to the motion of the source, which may be fast, the conventional frequency-domain PO method is not employed. Instead, a time-domain variant that explicitly accounts for the motion is formulated. The study focuses on the near-field regime, where source motion induces a complex wave field beyond a simple Doppler effect. The proposed algorithm accommodates arbitrary source trajectories and arbitrary signal bandwidth, as well as high source velocities relative to the speed of sound. Near and beyond the shadow boundary, where PO is least accurate, the formulation is supplemented by a Fock correction of the surface field, extended here to a moving source with a harmonic signal. The method matches or exceeds the accuracy of finite element simulations at a computational cost lower by orders of magnitude.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15823)

{S:34}Realizing non-Hermitian tunneling phenomena using non-reciprocal active acoustic metamaterials
@before Supersonic wave propagation
[Non-reciprocal systems have been shown to exhibit various interesting wave phenomena, such as the non-Hermitian skin effect, which causes accumulation of modes at boundaries. Recent research on discrete systems showed that this effect can pose a barrier for waves hitting an interface between reciprocal and non-reciprocal systems. Under certain conditions, however, waves can tunnel through this barrier, similar to the tunneling of particles in quantum mechanics. This work proposes and investigates an active acoustic metamaterial design to realize this tunneling phenomenon in the acoustical wave domain. The metamaterial consists of an acoustic waveguide with microphones and loudspeakers embedded in its wall. Starting from a purely discrete non-Hermitian lattice model of the system, a hybrid continuous-discrete acoustic model is derived, resulting in distributed feedback control laws to realize the desired behavior for acoustic waves. The proposed control laws are validated using frequency and time domain finite element method simulations, which include lumped electro-acoustic loudspeaker models. Additionally, an experimental demonstration is performed using a waveguide with embedded active unit cells and a digital implementation of the control laws. In both the simulations and experiments the tunneling phenomenon is successfully observed.](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/158/6/4900/3375299)
